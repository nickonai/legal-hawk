# VAT/GST Tax Compliance for SaaS Businesses

## Overview

SaaS businesses selling internationally face a complex web of indirect tax obligations. Digital services (including SaaS subscriptions, online courses, and digital content) are taxable in most jurisdictions, often from the very first sale. This reference covers VAT, GST, and sales tax requirements across major markets, merchant of record models, and practical compliance guidance.

---

## European Union -- VAT One Stop Shop (OSS)

### Thresholds and Rates

- **Threshold for non-EU sellers**: EUR 0 -- VAT collection is mandatory from the first B2C sale to any EU customer.
- **Threshold for EU sellers selling cross-border**: EUR 10,000 aggregate cross-border B2C sales within the EU. Below this threshold, the seller may charge their home country VAT rate. Above it, the destination country rate applies.
- **VAT rates by country** (standard rates for digital services):

| Country | VAT Rate | Country | VAT Rate |
|---------|----------|---------|----------|
| Luxembourg | 17% | Germany | 19% |
| Malta | 18% | Austria | 20% |
| Cyprus | 19% | Belgium | 21% |
| France | 20% | Czech Republic | 21% |
| Bulgaria | 20% | Latvia | 21% |
| Estonia | 22% | Lithuania | 21% |
| Italy | 22% | Netherlands | 21% |
| Slovenia | 22% | Spain | 21% |
| Finland | 25.5% | Ireland | 23% |
| Denmark | 25% | Poland | 23% |
| Sweden | 25% | Portugal | 23% |
| Croatia | 25% | Romania | 19% |
| Hungary | 27% | Slovakia | 23% |
| Greece | 24% | | |

### OSS Registration and Filing

- **Non-Union OSS scheme**: Available to non-EU businesses. Register in one EU member state and file a single quarterly return covering all EU B2C digital service sales.
- **Union OSS scheme**: For EU-established businesses selling cross-border B2C digital services. Single registration in home member state.
- **Filing frequency**: Quarterly returns due by the end of the month following the quarter (e.g., Q1 return due April 30).
- **Payment**: Due at the same time as the filing. Payment must be in EUR.
- **Record keeping**: Retain records for 10 years. Must keep at least two non-contradictory pieces of evidence of customer location (IP address, billing address, bank country, SIM country, etc.).

### B2B Reverse Charge

- When selling to EU business customers who provide a valid VAT identification number, VAT is not charged by the seller. Instead, the buyer self-assesses under the reverse charge mechanism.
- **Validate VAT numbers** via the EU VIES system before applying zero-rate.
- If the buyer does not provide a valid VAT number, treat the sale as B2C and charge VAT.

### Invoice Requirements (EU)

- Seller name, address, VAT identification number
- Customer name and address
- Sequential invoice number
- Invoice date and supply date
- Description of the digital service
- Net amount, VAT rate, and VAT amount
- Total amount including VAT
- For reverse charge: notation "Reverse charge -- Article 196 Council Directive 2006/112/EC"
- For OSS: reference to the OSS scheme and the member state of consumption
- Invoices must be issued by the 15th of the month following the supply in most member states

## United Kingdom -- VAT

### Thresholds and Rates

- **Rate**: 20% standard rate for digital services.
- **Threshold for non-UK businesses**: GBP 0 -- from the first B2C digital service sale to a UK consumer, non-UK businesses must register for UK VAT and charge 20%.
- **Threshold for UK-established businesses**: GBP 90,000 (domestic VAT registration threshold as of April 2024). This applies to total taxable turnover, not just digital services.

### Registration and Filing

- Non-UK businesses selling digital services B2C must register directly with HMRC. There is no simplified one-stop scheme equivalent to the EU OSS for non-EU sellers post-Brexit.
- **Filing**: Quarterly VAT returns via Making Tax Digital (MTD). Returns due one month and seven days after the end of each quarter.
- **Payment**: Due at the same filing deadline. Direct debit adds three extra working days.

### B2B Reverse Charge

- Sales to UK VAT-registered businesses: reverse charge applies. The UK business self-assesses the VAT.
- Verify the customer's UK VAT number before zero-rating.

### Invoice Requirements (UK)

- Similar to EU requirements: seller details, buyer details, sequential number, date, description, net amount, VAT rate, VAT amount, gross amount.
- For reverse charge sales: "Reverse charge: customer to account for VAT to HMRC."

### Price Display

- **B2C**: Prices displayed to UK consumers must include VAT (VAT-inclusive pricing required under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Price Marking Order 2004).

## United Arab Emirates -- VAT

### Thresholds and Rates

- **Rate**: 5% VAT on digital services.
- **Threshold for non-UAE businesses**: AED 0 for B2C digital services -- mandatory registration from the first sale to a UAE consumer (no de minimis threshold for foreign digital service providers).
- **Domestic threshold**: AED 375,000 mandatory registration; AED 187,500 voluntary registration.

### Registration and Filing

- Non-resident providers of digital services to UAE consumers must register with the Federal Tax Authority (FTA).
- **Filing**: Quarterly returns (standard), though some businesses may be assigned monthly periods.
- **Payment**: Due within 28 days of the end of the tax period.

### Invoice Requirements (UAE)

- Must be issued in AED (though dual-currency display is permitted).
- Seller TRN (Tax Registration Number), buyer TRN if B2B, description, date, amounts.
- Simplified tax invoices permitted for supplies under AED 10,000.

## Russia -- NDS (VAT)

### Thresholds and Rates

- **Rate**: 20% NDS (НДС -- налог на добавленную стоимость).
- **"Google Tax"**: Since January 1, 2017, foreign companies providing digital services (including SaaS, online courses, digital content) to Russian consumers must register for Russian VAT and charge 20%. This applies from the very first sale -- there is no revenue threshold.
- **Scope**: Covers all "electronically supplied services" to Russian individuals and businesses.

### Registration and Filing

- Foreign digital service providers must register with the Federal Tax Service (ФНС) via a simplified online registration process.
- **Filing**: Quarterly VAT returns, due by the 25th of the month following the quarter.
- **Payment**: Due in three equal monthly installments following the quarter (25th of each month).

### Currency Requirements

- **Prices must be displayed in Russian rubles (RUB)** for Russian consumers. While payment can technically be processed in other currencies, the legal price must be denominated in rubles. This is required under Russian consumer protection law (Article 10, Law No. 2300-1 "On Protection of Consumer Rights").

### Invoice Requirements (Russia)

- For B2C digital services by foreign providers: simplified invoicing via the ФНС personal account.
- For B2B: standard Russian invoice (счёт-фактура) with all required fields including ИНН/КПП of both parties.

### ООО Tax Regimes (for Russia-based entities)

If operating through a Russian ООО (LLC equivalent):

- **ОСНО (General Tax System)**: Standard regime. Subject to 20% profit tax + 20% VAT. Required if annual revenue exceeds approximately RUB 265.8 million (2025 threshold) or if selling VAT-liable services and needing to issue VAT invoices to business customers.
- **УСН (Simplified Tax System)**: Available for smaller businesses. Two options:
  - **УСН "Доходы" (Revenue)**: 6% of gross revenue (some regions offer reduced rates down to 1%).
  - **УСН "Доходы минус расходы" (Revenue minus Expenses)**: 15% of net profit (minimum 1% of revenue). Some regions offer reduced rates down to 5%.
  - **УСН limitations**: Cannot exceed approximately RUB 265.8 million annual revenue (2025) or RUB 150 million in fixed assets. Cannot have more than 130 employees. **Important**: УСН businesses are generally not VAT payers, but starting January 1, 2025, УСН businesses with revenue above RUB 60 million must charge VAT (5% or 7% depending on revenue band, or standard 20%).

### UAE-Russia Double Tax Treaty (DTT)

- A new UAE-Russia Double Tax Treaty was signed and takes effect **January 1, 2026**.
- Relevant for SaaS businesses with entities in both jurisdictions.
- Key provisions: reduced withholding tax rates on royalties (which may apply to software licensing fees), elimination of double taxation on business profits.
- **Practical impact**: If a SaaS company has a UAE entity receiving income from Russian customers, the DTT may reduce or eliminate Russian withholding tax on certain payment types (royalties reduced to 0-5% depending on conditions). Consult a cross-border tax advisor for structuring.

## United States -- Sales Tax

### Post-Wayfair Economic Nexus

Following the 2018 Supreme Court decision in *South Dakota v. Wayfair*, states can require out-of-state sellers to collect sales tax if they exceed economic nexus thresholds, even without physical presence.

### Thresholds by State (Representative Examples)

| State | Revenue Threshold | Transaction Threshold | SaaS Taxable? |
|-------|------------------|-----------------------|---------------|
| South Dakota | $100,000 | 200 transactions | Yes |
| California | $500,000 | None | Generally no (service) |
| Texas | $500,000 | None | Yes (data processing) |
| New York | $500,000 | 100 transactions | Yes |
| Washington | $100,000 | None | Yes |
| Pennsylvania | $100,000 | None | Yes |
| Connecticut | $100,000 | 200 transactions | Yes |
| Massachusetts | $100,000 | None | Yes |
| Ohio | $100,000 | 200 transactions | Yes (as "automatic data processing") |
| Tennessee | $100,000 | None | Yes |
| Illinois | $100,000 | 200 transactions | Generally no (service, but rules evolving) |
| Florida | $100,000 | None | No (as of 2025) |

### SaaS Taxability -- Key Complexity

- **SaaS taxability varies dramatically by state**. There is no federal standard.
- Approximately 25-30 states tax SaaS as of 2025. The number is growing.
- States that tax SaaS typically classify it as: (a) tangible personal property, (b) data processing services, (c) a specifically enumerated digital product, or (d) electronically delivered software.
- States that do NOT tax SaaS often classify it as a non-taxable service.
- **B2B exemptions**: Some states offer exemptions for software used in business (e.g., resale exemption, manufacturing exemption). Collect exemption certificates (use Avalara CertCapture or similar).

### Filing and Registration

- Must register individually with each state where nexus is established.
- Filing frequencies vary: monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on revenue volume in each state.
- Use automated solutions: **Avalara**, **TaxJar** (by Stripe), **Anrok** for SaaS-specific calculations.

### Price Display (US)

- **Prices are displayed excluding tax**. Tax is added at checkout. This is standard practice and legally expected.

## India -- GST

### Thresholds and Rates

- **Rate**: 18% GST on OIDAR (Online Information and Database Access or Retrieval) services. SaaS subscriptions, online courses, and digital content are classified as OIDAR.
- **Threshold for foreign providers**: INR 20 lakh (~USD 2,400) aggregate turnover for OIDAR services to Indian consumers. Below this, no registration required.
- **Note**: If selling through an intermediary (marketplace), the intermediary may be liable for GST.

### Registration and Filing

- Foreign providers must register as OIDAR service providers under a simplified registration scheme with the GST authorities.
- **Filing**: Quarterly returns (GSTR-5A for OIDAR providers), due by the 20th of the month following the quarter.
- **Payment**: In INR, due with the return.

### B2B vs B2C

- **B2C to Indian consumers**: Foreign provider charges 18% GST.
- **B2B to Indian businesses**: Reverse charge mechanism applies. The Indian business self-assesses and pays GST.

## Australia -- GST

### Thresholds and Rates

- **Rate**: 10% GST on digital supplies (including SaaS, online courses, digital content).
- **Threshold**: AUD 75,000 annual turnover from sales to Australian consumers. Below this, no registration required.

### Registration and Filing

- Register for GST with the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) under the simplified registration system for non-resident suppliers.
- **Filing**: Quarterly Business Activity Statements (BAS), due 28 days after the end of each quarter.
- **Payment**: Due with the BAS filing, in AUD.

### Price Display

- Prices displayed to Australian consumers should include GST (GST-inclusive pricing is standard practice, required under the Australian Consumer Law for displayed prices).

## Merchant of Record (MoR) Models vs. Stripe Tax

### What is a Merchant of Record?

A Merchant of Record (MoR) is a third-party entity that becomes the legal seller of your product. The MoR handles tax collection, remittance, and compliance on your behalf. Your customer's credit card statement shows the MoR's name, not yours.

### MoR Platforms Comparison

| Platform | Fee | What They Handle | Key Considerations |
|----------|-----|------------------|--------------------|
| **Paddle** | ~5% of revenue | VAT/GST collection and remittance worldwide, invoicing, payment processing, chargeback handling, tax compliance in 200+ countries | Higher fee but truly hands-off. Paddle is the seller of record. Supports SaaS and digital products. Limited customization of checkout. |
| **Lemon Squeezy** | 4-5% of revenue (5% + $0.50 on plans under $10) | VAT/GST worldwide, invoicing, payment processing, fraud protection | Good for smaller SaaS/digital product businesses. Simpler interface. Now owned by Stripe. |
| **FastSpring** | 4-5% of revenue (varies by volume) | Global tax compliance, localized checkout, payment processing, chargeback management | Strong for software and SaaS. Good localization. Supports subscription management. |

### Stripe Tax (You Remain MoR)

- **Stripe Tax** is an add-on to Stripe Payments. Fee: 0.5% per transaction (on top of standard Stripe processing fees of ~2.9% + $0.30).
- **Critical difference**: With Stripe Tax, **you remain the Merchant of Record**. Stripe calculates and collects the correct tax amount, but **you are responsible for registering and filing** in each jurisdiction.
- Stripe Tax provides tax calculation and collection, but you must:
  - Register for VAT/GST/sales tax in each jurisdiction where you have obligations
  - File returns yourself (or use Stripe's filing partners)
  - Handle audits and inquiries from tax authorities
- **Total effective cost comparison**:
  - Stripe (processing) + Stripe Tax: ~3.4% + $0.30 per transaction, plus your own filing costs and time
  - Paddle: ~5% all-in, fully managed
  - Lemon Squeezy: ~5% all-in, fully managed
  - FastSpring: ~5% all-in, fully managed

### When to Use Each

- **Use a full MoR (Paddle/Lemon Squeezy/FastSpring)** if:
  - You sell to many countries and want zero tax compliance burden
  - You are a small team without tax/legal resources
  - The 5% fee is acceptable relative to your margins
  - You do not need deep checkout customization

- **Use Stripe + Stripe Tax** if:
  - You want more control over the checkout and customer experience
  - You have tax/legal resources or use a filing service
  - You are optimizing for lower per-transaction fees at scale
  - You sell primarily in a few jurisdictions where you are already registered

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

### EU VAT

- **Late filing**: Penalties vary by member state, typically EUR 50-500 per late return, escalating with repeated offenses.
- **Failure to register**: Back-assessment of VAT owed plus interest (typically 0.5-1% per month) and penalties of 10-30% of unpaid VAT.
- **Fraud/evasion**: Criminal penalties possible, including imprisonment in severe cases.

### UK VAT

- **Late registration**: Penalty up to 100% of the VAT due from the date registration should have been made.
- **Late filing**: Points-based penalty system (from January 2023). Accumulate points for each late filing; financial penalties apply after reaching the threshold (e.g., GBP 200 per late return after threshold).
- **Late payment**: Interest charged at Bank of England base rate + 2.5%. Additional penalties of 2-4% of outstanding tax.

### Russia NDS

- **Failure to register**: Fines of 10% of income earned during unregistered period (minimum RUB 40,000).
- **Late filing**: 5% of unpaid tax per month of delay (minimum RUB 1,000, maximum 30% of unpaid tax).
- **Non-payment**: 20% of unpaid tax (40% if willful).

### US Sales Tax

- **Failure to collect**: The seller becomes personally liable for uncollected tax plus interest and penalties.
- **Penalties vary by state**: Typically 5-25% of uncollected tax plus interest of 0.5-1.5% per month.
- **Voluntary Disclosure Agreements (VDAs)**: Most states offer VDA programs that waive penalties (but not tax owed or interest) for sellers who voluntarily come into compliance.

## Practical Compliance Checklist

1. **Determine where your customers are located** -- implement geolocation and collect billing addresses.
2. **Check taxability** in each jurisdiction for your specific product type (SaaS, digital course, digital content).
3. **Register** for VAT/GST/sales tax in every jurisdiction where you have obligations.
4. **Configure tax calculation** -- either through your MoR or via Stripe Tax / Avalara / TaxJar.
5. **Display prices correctly** -- VAT-inclusive for EU/UK B2C, tax-exclusive for US.
6. **Issue compliant invoices** meeting each jurisdiction's requirements.
7. **File returns on time** -- set calendar reminders for each jurisdiction's deadlines.
8. **Retain records** for the required period (10 years for EU, 6 years for UK, 5 years for most US states).
9. **Review thresholds annually** -- thresholds and rates change. Monitor for new tax obligations as you grow.
10. **Consider a full MoR** if managing multi-jurisdiction compliance is distracting from your core business.
