# Testimonials, Reviews & Marketing Claims: Legal Requirements

> Cross-jurisdictional reference for SaaS companies using customer testimonials, reviews, case studies, and performance claims in marketing materials. Covers the US (FTC), EU (Omnibus Directive + DSA), Russia (38-FZ + ORD), and UK (ASA/CAP Code).

---

## 1. FTC Endorsement Guides (United States, revised 2023)

### Governing Law

- **16 CFR Part 255** -- Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising
- **Section 5 of the FTC Act** (15 U.S.C. 45) -- Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices
- Revised guides effective **29 June 2023** (first major update since 2009)

### Core Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Honest opinion | Testimonials must reflect the honest opinions, findings, beliefs, or experience of the endorser (16 CFR 255.1(a)) |
| Typical results | Advertisers may no longer use "results not typical" disclaimers alone. Must either show **typical results** consumers can expect, or clearly and conspicuously disclose the generally expected performance (16 CFR 255.2(b)) |
| Material connections | **All** material connections between endorser and advertiser must be disclosed -- payment, free products, employment, family relationships, social media partnerships (16 CFR 255.5) |
| Substantiation | Claims made through endorsements require the same level of substantiation as if the advertiser made the claim directly (16 CFR 255.2(a)) |

### Income & Earnings Claims

- Written substantiation must be maintained and available for inspection
- Records must be kept for a minimum of **3 years**
- Must reflect **typical** participant results, not outlier outcomes
- If using specific dollar figures, must be able to prove the stated amount is representative
- Atypical results require clear, prominent disclosure of what a typical participant achieves

### Paid Testimonials & Influencer Disclosure

- "Material connection" includes: monetary payment, free products/services, discounts, affiliate commissions, contest entries, employment relationships, personal relationships
- Disclosure must be **clear and conspicuous** -- not buried in hashtags, below the fold, or in small print
- Social media: disclosure should appear at the **beginning** of the post or in the video itself, not just in a description
- Platform-native disclosure tools (e.g., "Paid partnership" labels) may satisfy the requirement if genuinely visible
- The advertiser is **liable** for failure to disclose, not just the endorser

### Before/After Claims

- No deceptive editing or manipulation of images
- Images must be current and representative of actual results
- Must not create false impressions about achievable outcomes
- Digital alteration must be disclosed if it materially affects the impression conveyed

### AI-Generated Reviews

- AI-generated reviews presented as authentic consumer experiences are **prohibited** without disclosure
- FTC enforcement action: **FTC v. Rytr** (2023) -- FTC sued an AI writing tool for enabling and facilitating fake review generation
- The FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (finalized 2024) specifically bans:
  - Fake or false consumer reviews
  - Buying positive or negative reviews
  - Insider reviews without disclosure
  - Company-controlled review websites that appear independent
  - Review suppression through improper means
  - Misuse of fake social media indicators (followers, likes)

### Penalties

| Violation Type | Maximum Penalty |
|---|---|
| Per violation of FTC Act Section 5 | **$51,744** per violation (adjusted annually for inflation) |
| Consumer redress | Full disgorgement of ill-gotten gains |
| Injunctive relief | Court-ordered cessation + corrective advertising |
| Knowing violations | Penalties may be tripled under aggravating circumstances |

### Key FTC Enforcement Examples

- **FTC v. Sunday Riley** (2019) -- company directed employees to post fake Sephora reviews; consent order issued
- **FTC v. Fashion Nova** (2022) -- $4.2M settlement for suppressing negative reviews (1-3 star ratings blocked)
- **FTC v. Rytr** (2023) -- AI review generation platform sued for facilitating fake reviews

## 2. EU Omnibus Directive + Digital Services Act (DSA)

### Governing Law

- **Directive (EU) 2019/2161** (Omnibus Directive) -- amending four consumer protection directives
- **Regulation (EU) 2022/2065** (Digital Services Act)
- **Directive 2005/29/EC** (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, as amended)
- **Annex I** of UCPD -- "blacklisted" practices that are unfair in all circumstances

### Core Prohibitions

| Practice | Status |
|---|---|
| Fake reviews | **Banned** (Annex I, Item 23b UCPD) |
| Claiming reviews are from verified purchasers when no verification exists | **Banned** (Annex I, Item 23b UCPD) |
| Submitting or commissioning fake reviews/endorsements | **Banned** (Annex I, Item 23c UCPD) |
| Manipulating reviews (selectively publishing, deleting negative reviews) | **Banned** under general unfairness provisions |
| Undisclosed incentivized reviews | **Banned** -- all incentives must be disclosed |

### Review Verification Requirements

- Traders who provide access to consumer reviews must inform consumers whether and how they ensure reviews come from consumers who actually used or purchased the product (Article 7(6) UCPD as amended)
- If a trader claims reviews are verified, they must use **reasonable and proportionate** steps to verify authenticity
- No obligation to publish reviews, but if published, must not selectively suppress negative ones

### Penalties by Member State

| Jurisdiction | Maximum Penalty |
|---|---|
| EU-wide framework | Up to **4% of annual turnover** or **EUR 2 million** (whichever is higher) for cross-border infringements under CPC Regulation |
| France (Code de la consommation) | Up to **EUR 1,500,000** for a legal person; up to 10% of average annual turnover; up to 50% of expenditure on the practice |
| Germany (UWG -- Gesetz gegen den unlauteren Wettbewerb) | Up to **EUR 50,000** per violation (administrative); unlimited civil liability for damages and injunctive relief |
| Italy (Codice del Consumo) | Up to **EUR 10,000,000** (AGCM enforcement) |
| Spain (Ley de Competencia Desleal + LGDCU) | Up to **EUR 100,000** for minor; up to **EUR 1,000,000** for serious infractions |
| Netherlands (ACM enforcement) | Up to **EUR 900,000** or 1% of turnover |

### Digital Services Act Additions (effective February 2024)

- Very large online platforms (VLOPs) must provide transparency on commercial communications
- Online marketplaces must ensure traceability of traders
- Risk assessments must address manipulation, including via fake reviews
- Penalties for DSA violations: up to **6% of global annual turnover**

## 3. Russia -- Federal Law on Advertising (38-FZ)

### Governing Law

- **Federal Law No. 38-FZ of 13.03.2006** "On Advertising" (O Reklame)
- **Federal Law No. 149-FZ** "On Information, Information Technologies and Protection of Information"
- **Government Decree No. 1982 of 25.11.2022** (implementing ORD system)
- Enforced by **FAS** (Federal Antimonopoly Service) and **Roskomnadzor**

### Misleading Advertising (Article 5, 38-FZ)

| Violation | Penalty Range |
|---|---|
| Misleading advertising (legal entities) | **100,000 -- 500,000 RUB** |
| Misleading advertising (officials) | **4,000 -- 20,000 RUB** |
| Violation of advertising law (general, legal entities) | **100,000 -- 500,000 RUB** (Article 14.3 KoAP RF) |
| Repeat violations | Penalties may double; possible suspension of advertising activities |

### Income Claims in Educational Advertising

- Claims about potential earnings from educational courses must be substantiated with evidence
- FAS actively enforces against misleading income promises in EdTech and online courses
- Required: disclosure of typical outcomes, not exceptional cases
- Statistical substantiation must be available upon request

### ORD System (Online Advertising Registry)

See Section 6 below for full ORD details.

## 4. UK -- ASA/CAP Code

### Governing Framework

- **UK Code of Non-broadcast Advertising and Direct & Promotional Marketing** (CAP Code, 12th Edition)
- **Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008** (CPRs)
- **Competition and Markets Authority** (CMA) guidance on online reviews and endorsements
- Administered by the **Advertising Standards Authority** (ASA)

### Key Rules on Testimonials

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Rule 3.45** | Testimonials or endorsements must relate to the product being advertised |
| **Rule 3.47** | Marketers must hold **documentary evidence** that a testimonial is genuine and hold contact details for the person who gave it |
| **Rule 3.48** | Testimonials must be relevant to the claims being made and must not contain any hidden commercial interests (e.g., the endorser is a shareholder) |
| **Rule 3.49** | **Objective claims** that appear in a testimonial (e.g., "I lost 10kg") must be substantiated with **independent evidence** |
| **Rule 3.50** | Marketers must obtain **explicit consent** from the individual before using their testimonial |

### Additional CAP Requirements

- **Rule 11.2**: Marketing communications must not falsely claim that the marketer is acting as a consumer or for purposes outside their business
- **Rule 11.3**: Traders must identify themselves clearly; hidden commercial intent is prohibited
- **Rule 3.7**: Substantiation must be available before a claim is published, not after
- **Section 22 (Affiliate marketing)**: All affiliates must disclose the commercial nature of the relationship

### Enforcement Model

- ASA is a **self-regulatory** body -- no direct power to impose fines
- Non-compliant advertisers are:
  - Named publicly on the ASA website ("Ad Alerts")
  - Referred to the **CMA** for potential legal enforcement
  - Subject to sanctions through media owners (ad space withdrawal)
  - Referred to **Ofcom** (for broadcast) or **Trading Standards** (for CPR breaches)
- CMA can pursue cases under CPRs with penalties of up to **unlimited fines** and up to **2 years imprisonment** for individuals

### CMA Guidance on Online Reviews (2022)

- Businesses must not commission or incentivize fake reviews
- Hosting platforms must take reasonable steps to detect and remove fake reviews
- Suppression of genuine negative reviews may constitute an unfair commercial practice
- CMA has enforcement powers under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024

## 5. Specific Marketing Practices

### 5.1 "Results Not Typical" Disclaimers

**When required:**
- Whenever a testimonial features results that do not represent what consumers can generally expect
- Any income, weight loss, performance, or outcome claim that is above average

**What to include:**
- The generally expected results in the same context
- Specific quantified typical outcomes (e.g., "Most participants earn between $200-$500/month. The testimonial above represents a top 5% earner.")
- Clear, prominent placement -- not in footnotes or small print

**How to display (FTC standard):**
- Same font size and prominence as the testimonial itself
- Adjacent to or immediately following the claim
- Not requiring scrolling, clicking, or hovering to view
- In video/audio: spoken aloud or displayed for sufficient reading time

**EU approach:**
- Under the Omnibus Directive, misleading use of testimonials constitutes an unfair commercial practice regardless of disclaimers
- Disclaimers do not cure a fundamentally misleading presentation

**UK approach:**
- CAP Rule 3.49 requires independent substantiation of objective claims
- Disclaimers alone do not satisfy the substantiation burden

### 5.2 Affiliate Disclosure

**FTC (US):**
- Clear and conspicuous disclosure of affiliate relationship (16 CFR 255.5)
- Must appear before the consumer engages with the affiliate link
- Acceptable: "I earn a commission if you purchase through this link"
- Unacceptable: "#ad" buried at the end of 30 hashtags

**UK CAP:**
- Section 22 of the CAP Code: affiliate nature must be immediately apparent
- ASA rulings: "AD" label should be immediately visible without clicking through
- Affiliate comparison websites must disclose that they receive commissions

**EU Omnibus:**
- Failure to disclose commercial intent = misleading omission under Article 7 UCPD
- Each member state may impose additional disclosure requirements
- Germany (TMG + DDG): "Werbung" or "Anzeige" label required for paid content

### 5.3 Case Studies

**Consent requirements:**
- Written consent from the individual or organization featured
- Specify scope of use (channels, duration, territories)
- UK: explicit consent required under CAP Rule 3.50
- EU: consider GDPR implications for personal data in case studies (legal basis required)
- US: model releases for image use; written testimonial releases

**Privacy considerations:**
- GDPR Article 6 -- lawful basis for processing (typically consent under Art. 6(1)(a))
- Right to withdraw consent at any time (Art. 7(3) GDPR)
- If case study includes personal data, a privacy notice must be provided
- Consider data minimization -- only include necessary personal details

**Incentive disclosure:**
- If the case study participant received any benefit (discount, free service, payment), this must be disclosed
- FTC: material connection disclosure applies
- EU: incentivized endorsements must be labeled

### 5.4 "As Featured In" Claims

- Must be **truthful** -- the product or company was actually featured in the cited publication
- Must be **current** -- outdated media mentions should be dated or removed if misleading
- Cannot imply endorsement if the coverage was neutral, negative, or merely factual
- Using a publication's logo may require **trademark permission** from the publication
- FTC: misleading "as seen on" claims are deceptive under Section 5
- UK: CAP Rule 3.7 requires substantiation; ASA has ruled against misleading media claims

### 5.5 Stock Photos vs. Real Clients

- **Never** imply that stock photos depict real customers, users, or case study participants
- If using stock imagery alongside testimonials, clearly label: "Image used for illustration purposes only" or similar
- FTC: misrepresenting stock models as actual customers = deceptive practice
- EU UCPD: creating false impression of real customer experience = unfair commercial practice
- UK: CAP Rule 3.1 requires ads not to materially mislead by implication

### 5.6 Fake Urgency and Scarcity

**"Only 3 spots left" / "Limited availability":**
- **Illegal if false** across all major jurisdictions
- EU: Annex I, Item 7 of the UCPD -- "falsely stating that a product will only be available for a very limited time, or that it will only be available on particular terms for a very limited time, in order to elicit an immediate decision" = **blacklisted practice** (unfair in all circumstances)
- US: FTC Section 5 -- false scarcity or urgency claims are deceptive
- UK: Blacklisted practice under Schedule 1, Paragraph 7 of the CPRs 2008

**Countdown timers:**
- **Legal** if the timer reflects a genuine deadline (e.g., a real sale ending at a specific time)
- **Illegal** if the timer resets, is fabricated, or creates artificial urgency
- EU: fake countdown timers specifically mentioned in enforcement guidance as a dark pattern
- Digital Services Act: platforms must not use deceptive design patterns including fake urgency

**Enforcement examples:**
- Italy AGCM fined multiple companies for fake "last room available" claims in travel booking
- CMA (UK) investigated hotel booking platforms for misleading urgency messaging

### 5.7 Earnings Disclaimers for Online Courses and Programs

**FTC requirements:**
- Must provide truthful, non-misleading income representations
- If any income claims are made, must disclose what participants **typically** earn
- FTC Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR 437): requires written earnings claims substantiation document
- Must be based on verifiable data, not hypothetical scenarios
- The disclaimer must be as prominent as the earnings claim itself

**What to include:**
- Median or average participant earnings
- Percentage of participants who achieve the claimed results
- Time period over which results were measured
- Any conditions or prerequisites for achieving the stated results
- Statement that results are not guaranteed and depend on individual effort

**Format example:**
> "The income figures stated are our personal results and/or the results of select students. The average participant who completes the program earns $X in their first 12 months. Results vary and are not guaranteed. See our full earnings disclosure at [link]."

**FTC enforcement:**
- FTC v. Agora Financial (2020) -- $2M+ settlement for misleading income claims in investment courses
- FTC v. Traffic Jam Events (2021) -- challenged for false earnings representations in business coaching

## 6. ORD -- Russian Online Advertising Labeling System

### Background

Effective **1 September 2022**, Russia implemented a mandatory online advertising registration and labeling system under amendments to Federal Law No. 38-FZ "On Advertising" and Federal Law No. 149-FZ.

### How It Works

1. **Registration with ORS (Operator of Advertising Systems):**
   - All participants in the online advertising chain (advertisers, agencies, ad platforms, publishers) must register with an authorized ORS (Operator of Advertising Systems)
   - ORS operators are approved by Roskomnadzor

2. **Unique erid identifier:**
   - Every piece of online advertising must be assigned a unique tracking token called an **erid** (electronic resource identifier)
   - The erid is obtained from the ORD (Operator of Advertising Data) before the ad is published
   - Format: alphanumeric string (e.g., `erid: 2VtzqvBMFZb`)

3. **"Reklama" label:**
   - Every online ad must carry the label **"Reклама"** (Advertising) prominently displayed
   - Must also display the advertiser's name or trademark, along with the erid
   - Label must be visible without additional clicks or actions

4. **Data reporting to Roskomnadzor:**
   - All advertising data (creative content, targeting parameters, placement details, advertiser identity, contracts, costs) must be reported to Roskomnadzor through the ORD within **30 calendar days** of the ad placement
   - Data is stored in ERIR (Unified Register of Internet Advertising) maintained by Roskomnadzor

### Who Must Comply

- Advertisers (brands and businesses)
- Advertising agencies and intermediaries
- Advertising platforms and ad networks
- Website publishers and bloggers
- Social media creators who publish paid content

### Exemptions

- Email communications sent to the sender's own subscribers
- Push notifications from a company's own app
- Organic social media posts that are not paid promotion
- Ads placed solely outside the Russian Federation (with no Russian-targeted distribution)

### Penalties (as of 2024 amendments)

| Violation | Penalty (Legal Entities) | Penalty (Officials) |
|---|---|---|
| Failure to label advertising (no "Reklama" or erid) | **100,000 -- 500,000 RUB** | **30,000 -- 100,000 RUB** |
| Failure to submit advertising data to ORD/Roskomnadzor | **200,000 -- 700,000 RUB** | **30,000 -- 100,000 RUB** |
| Providing incomplete or inaccurate data | **100,000 -- 300,000 RUB** | **10,000 -- 30,000 RUB** |
| Violations by ORS/ORD operators | **300,000 -- 700,000 RUB** | **30,000 -- 100,000 RUB** |
| Repeat violations | Penalties may be doubled | Administrative suspension possible |

### Practical Compliance Steps

1. Register with an authorized ORD operator
2. Obtain an erid for each advertising creative before publication
3. Add "Reклама" label and erid to all online advertising materials
4. Include advertiser name/brand alongside the label
5. Submit all required data to the ORD within the 30-day reporting window
6. Retain records of all advertising placements, contracts, and creatives
7. Monitor regulatory updates -- FAS and Roskomnadzor issue regular clarifications

## 7. Testimonial & Marketing Compliance Audit Checklist

Use this checklist to verify compliance before publishing testimonials, reviews, case studies, or marketing claims.

### General Testimonial Compliance

- [ ] All testimonials reflect genuine, honest opinions of real customers
- [ ] Documentary evidence of authenticity is on file for every testimonial (name, contact details, proof of purchase/use)
- [ ] Written consent obtained from every testimonial provider, specifying scope of use
- [ ] All material connections (payment, free products, discounts, affiliate relationships) are clearly disclosed
- [ ] Disclosures are clear, conspicuous, and placed adjacent to the testimonial
- [ ] No AI-generated reviews are presented as authentic consumer experiences without disclosure
- [ ] No fake, fabricated, or purchased reviews are used anywhere in marketing materials

### Results and Performance Claims

- [ ] Testimonials showing specific results include disclosure of **typical** outcomes for consumers
- [ ] Income or earnings claims are substantiated with verifiable data kept on file for 3+ years
- [ ] "Before and after" materials are unedited (or editing is disclosed) and current
- [ ] No misleading implications created by selective presentation of exceptional results
- [ ] Earnings disclaimers for courses/programs include median results, percentage achieving outcomes, and time period

### Affiliate and Influencer Marketing

- [ ] All affiliate relationships are disclosed before the consumer encounters affiliate links
- [ ] Influencer disclosures meet FTC "clear and conspicuous" standard (visible, readable, early placement)
- [ ] Affiliate comparison sites disclose commission relationships
- [ ] Social media paid partnerships use visible labels (not buried in hashtags)
- [ ] Contracts with influencers/affiliates include compliance obligations for disclosure

### Case Studies

- [ ] Written consent obtained, specifying channels, duration, and territories of use
- [ ] GDPR-compliant privacy notice provided (if personal data of EU/UK residents is used)
- [ ] Legal basis for processing personal data documented (typically explicit consent)
- [ ] Any incentives provided to case study participants are disclosed
- [ ] Case study participants informed of their right to withdraw consent

### Review Management

- [ ] No suppression or selective publication of negative reviews
- [ ] If claiming reviews are "verified," a reasonable verification process is in place
- [ ] No employees posting reviews without disclosing their employment
- [ ] No incentivizing reviews without requiring disclosure of the incentive
- [ ] Review solicitation process does not bias toward positive responses

### Urgency and Scarcity Claims

- [ ] "Limited time" claims reflect genuine deadlines
- [ ] "Limited availability" or "only X left" claims are truthful and updated in real time
- [ ] Countdown timers reflect actual deadlines and do not reset or fabricate urgency
- [ ] No false statements about product availability or offer duration

### "As Featured In" and Media Claims

- [ ] All "as featured in" claims are truthful and verifiable
- [ ] Media mentions are current or clearly dated
- [ ] Publication logos are used with appropriate permission
- [ ] Coverage is not misrepresented as endorsement when it was neutral or factual

### Stock Imagery

- [ ] Stock photos are not presented as or implied to be real customers
- [ ] If stock images accompany testimonials, a clear disclaimer is present

### Russian ORD Compliance (if advertising in Russia)

- [ ] Registered with an authorized ORD operator
- [ ] Every online ad has a unique erid identifier
- [ ] "Reklama" label is prominently displayed on all online advertising
- [ ] Advertiser name or trademark is shown alongside the label
- [ ] Advertising data is submitted to ORD within 30 calendar days of placement
- [ ] Records of all advertising placements and contracts are retained

### Jurisdiction-Specific Final Checks

- [ ] US: FTC Endorsement Guide requirements reviewed; substantiation files current
- [ ] EU: Omnibus Directive compliance verified; no blacklisted practices (UCPD Annex I)
- [ ] UK: CAP Code Rules 3.45-3.50 satisfied; documentary evidence on file
- [ ] Russia: 38-FZ advertising requirements met; ORD labeling complete
- [ ] Multi-jurisdictional: claims reviewed under the strictest applicable standard
