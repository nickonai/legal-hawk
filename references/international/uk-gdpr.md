# UK GDPR -- Post-Brexit Data Protection for SaaS Companies

> **UK General Data Protection Regulation** (retained EU law, as amended by the Data Protection Act 2018 and the Data Protection, Privacy and Electronic Communications (Amendments etc) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019)
> Enforced by: **Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)**
> Applies to: processing of personal data of UK residents

---

## 1. Overview -- UK GDPR vs. EU GDPR

After Brexit (31 January 2020, transition ended 31 December 2020), the UK retained GDPR as domestic law ("UK GDPR"), supplemented by the Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA 2018). The core principles remain largely identical, but divergences exist and are growing.

### What stayed the same:
- Seven data protection principles (lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, accountability).
- Six lawful bases for processing (Art. 6 equivalents).
- Data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection).
- Breach notification within 72 hours.
- DPIA requirements.
- DPO appointment criteria.
- Data Processing Agreement requirements.
- Penalties structure (two tiers).

---

## 2. Key Differences from EU GDPR

| Area | EU GDPR | UK GDPR |
|---|---|---|
| **Supervisory authority** | National DPA of each member state | **ICO** (single authority) |
| **Lead supervisory authority** | One-stop-shop mechanism across EU | Not applicable -- ICO only |
| **Representative** | Art. 27 EU Representative | Art. 27 **UK Representative** (separate requirement) |
| **International transfers** | EU adequacy decisions, EU SCCs | UK adequacy regulations, **UK IDTA** or **UK Addendum to EU SCCs** |
| **Adequacy status** | EU determines adequacy of third countries | **UK determines its own** adequacy list (currently recognises EEA + additional countries) |
| **Age of consent (children)** | 16 years (member states may lower to 13) | **13 years** (DPA 2018 s.9) |
| **Immigration exemption** | Not present | UK GDPR allows exemption for immigration data (DPA 2018 Sch.2 Para.4) |
| **National security** | Limited exemptions | Broader national security exemptions |
| **Freedom of expression** | Deferred to member states | DPA 2018 s.15 -- journalism, literature, art exemption |
| **Penalty calculations** | % of global turnover of "undertaking" | Same formula but ICO interprets independently |
| **Certification schemes** | EU-approved certification bodies | UK-approved certification bodies |
| **Cookie rules** | ePrivacy Directive (national implementations) | **PECR** (Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003) |
| **Upcoming reform** | ePrivacy Regulation (pending) | **Data Protection and Digital Information Act** proposals (evolving) |

## 3. ICO Enforcement

### The ICO can:
- Issue **information notices** (requiring information).
- Issue **assessment notices** (to audit organisations).
- Issue **enforcement notices** (requiring specific actions).
- Issue **penalty notices** (monetary fines).
- Pursue **criminal prosecution** for certain offences.

### Penalty tiers:
| Tier | Maximum | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Lower tier** | Up to **GBP 8.7 million** or **2% of global annual turnover** (whichever is higher) | Obligations of controllers/processors, certification bodies, monitoring bodies |
| **Higher tier** | Up to **GBP 17.5 million** or **4% of global annual turnover** (whichever is higher) | Infringements of principles, lawful bases, data subject rights, international transfers |

### ICO enforcement approach:
- Generally seen as more pragmatic/proportionate than some EU DPAs.
- Publishes detailed guidance and codes of practice.
- Issues reprimands and enforcement notices before fines in many cases.
- Operates a **regulatory sandbox** for innovative data use.
- Maintains a public register of enforcement actions.

## 4. UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA)

Since the UK is no longer part of the EU, the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) do not work for UK transfers. The UK has its own mechanism.

### Options for UK international transfers:

**Option A: UK IDTA (International Data Transfer Agreement)**
- Standalone agreement approved by the ICO.
- Mandatory clauses that cannot be altered.
- Tables to fill in: parties, description of transfer, security measures.
- Must conduct a **Transfer Risk Assessment (TRA)** -- UK equivalent of TIA.

**Option B: UK Addendum to EU SCCs**
- Use the EU 2021 SCCs with the **UK International Data Transfer Addendum** attached.
- Addendum modifies the EU SCCs to work under UK law.
- Useful when you already have EU SCCs in place -- just add the Addendum.

### Transfer Risk Assessment (TRA):
- Assess the laws and practices of the destination country.
- Consider the specific circumstances of the transfer.
- Evaluate whether supplementary measures are needed.
- Document the assessment and review periodically.

### UK Adequacy Decisions:
The UK has granted adequacy to:
- All EEA/EU member states.
- Countries previously recognised as adequate by the EU (e.g., Japan, New Zealand, Canada, South Korea, Switzerland, Israel, Argentina, Uruguay).
- The United States (under the **UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework**).
- Additional countries may be added.

## 5. PECR -- Cookie Rules

The **Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003** (as amended) govern cookies and electronic marketing in the UK.

### Cookie consent:
- **Prior consent required** for non-essential cookies (same as ePrivacy Directive).
- Consent must be **informed, specific, and freely given**.
- **Strictly necessary cookies** are exempt (e.g., session cookies, security cookies, load balancing).
- Analytics cookies **require consent** (even first-party analytics).
- No pre-ticked boxes.

### Key PECR provisions:
| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Cookies/trackers** | Consent before setting (except strictly necessary) |
| **Direct marketing (email/SMS)** | Opt-in consent required (soft opt-in exception for existing customers) |
| **Direct marketing (phone)** | Must check TPS register; specific consent for automated calls |
| **Marketing fax** | Opt-in consent required |
| **Caller ID** | Must not conceal identity |
| **Traffic/location data** | Consent for value-added services using this data |

### Soft opt-in (existing customer exception):
- Applies only to **email and SMS** marketing.
- Customer's details obtained during a **sale or negotiation of a sale**.
- Marketing relates to **similar products or services**.
- Customer given a **simple opt-out** at the point of collection and in every subsequent message.
- Does NOT apply to: new prospects, third-party marketing, non-commercial messages.

### ICO guidance on cookies:
- ICO explicitly states that implied consent (continuing to browse) is **not sufficient**.
- Cookie walls that block access unless all cookies are accepted are **not compliant**.
- The ICO has signalled increased enforcement of cookie compliance.

## 6. UK Representative Requirement

### When required:
If a controller or processor:
- Is **not established in the UK**, AND
- Processes personal data of **UK data subjects** in connection with offering goods/services or monitoring behaviour.

### Requirements:
- Must designate a **UK Representative** in writing.
- Representative must be established in the UK.
- Representative acts as a contact point for the ICO and data subjects.
- Representative details must be included in the privacy policy.
- The representative's name and contact details must be communicated to the ICO.

### Exemptions:
- Public authorities.
- Occasional processing that does not include large-scale special category data or criminal conviction data, and is unlikely to result in risk.

### Important: EU Representative is NOT sufficient for UK
- If you need both EU and UK coverage, you need **two separate representatives**: one in the EU (Art. 27 EU GDPR) and one in the UK (Art. 27 UK GDPR).

## 7. Practical Differences for SaaS Companies

### Dual compliance scenario:
Most SaaS companies serving both EU and UK users must comply with **both** EU GDPR and UK GDPR. Key practical implications:

| Task | Action Required |
|---|---|
| **Privacy Policy** | Reference both EU GDPR and UK GDPR; list both supervisory authorities |
| **Representative** | Appoint EU Representative AND UK Representative (if not established in either) |
| **DPA** | May need separate UK and EU DPA provisions, or a single DPA covering both regimes |
| **International transfers** | Use EU SCCs for EU transfers + UK IDTA or UK Addendum for UK transfers |
| **Breach notification** | Notify ICO separately from EU lead supervisory authority (two notifications) |
| **DPIA** | Single DPIA can cover both regimes if it addresses requirements of each |
| **Cookie consent** | PECR for UK; national ePrivacy implementation for each EU state (largely similar in practice) |
| **Children's age** | 13 in UK; varies by EU member state (13-16) |

### Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code):
- UK-specific code of practice (DPA 2018 s.123).
- 15 standards for online services likely to be accessed by children.
- Applies to **information society services** likely to be accessed by children under 18.
- Key requirements: best interests of child, high privacy by default, data minimisation, no nudge techniques, no profiling by default.
- ICO can take enforcement action for non-compliance.

### UK Data Protection and Digital Information Act:
- Proposed reforms (status evolving -- check current status).
- Potential changes: recognised legitimate interests (no balancing test for certain activities), reduced ROPA obligations for low-risk processing, changes to cookie consent for analytics, reforms to Subject Access Request regime.
- SaaS companies should monitor but continue complying with current UK GDPR until any Act is passed and in force.

## 8. Compliance Checklist -- UK-Specific Items

### If serving UK users (in addition to general GDPR compliance):
- [ ] Appoint a **UK Representative** (if not established in the UK).
- [ ] Register with the **ICO** and pay the data protection fee (annual, based on size/turnover).
- [ ] Reference **UK GDPR and DPA 2018** in privacy policy and DPA.
- [ ] Ensure cookie consent mechanism is **PECR-compliant**.
- [ ] Use **UK IDTA or UK Addendum** for transfers out of the UK.
- [ ] Conduct **Transfer Risk Assessment** for UK data transfers.
- [ ] Set children's consent age to **13** for UK users.
- [ ] Comply with the **Children's Code** if service is likely to be accessed by under-18s.
- [ ] Know the **ICO** as the supervisory authority for UK-related matters.
- [ ] Prepare for separate **breach notification to ICO** (in addition to EU DPA if applicable).
- [ ] Monitor the **Data Protection and Digital Information Act** proposals for future changes.
